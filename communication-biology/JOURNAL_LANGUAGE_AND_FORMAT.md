# Communications Biology language and format profile

This profile was derived from the official guide and full-text review of the 20 closest Communications Biology papers identified for the project.

## Article language

- Lead with the biological problem, not the tool or an inventory of modules.
- Move from established context to a narrowly stated unresolved question.
- End the Introduction with what was done, the main findings and their interpretation.
- Use result subheadings as claims rather than topic labels.
- Begin each result with the question or comparison and then report quantitative evidence.
- Distinguish observation (`showed`, `measured`, `was associated with`) from inference (`supports`, `is consistent with`, `suggests`).
- State the domain of validity close to model-based claims.
- Use `Here we show` or an equivalent construction in the abstract, but avoid `novel`, `first`, `unprecedented`, `remarkable` and other novelty adjectives.
- Use an extended Discussion that relates findings to the literature and analyzes limitations; do not end with a summary-only conclusion.
- Prefer direct, concrete sentences and define specialist abbreviations at first use.

## Required or recommended proportions

- title: 15 words or fewer;
- abstract: 150 words or fewer;
- Introduction + Results + Discussion: approximately 5,000 words;
- main display items: approximately five for 2,500 words and no more than ten for approximately 5,000 words;
- Methods: no fixed word limit, but sufficient detail for reproduction;
- Supplementary Information: one self-contained PDF, preferably below 50 MB per file.

## Typography and figures

No submission-body font family, body font size or author-imposed two-column proportion is mandated. This draft uses 11-pt type on A4 paper with 25-mm margins for review readability. Published-page typography is applied by the journal after acceptance.

For figures, the journal specifies Arial or Helvetica used consistently, approximately 8-pt labels at final size, lines no thinner than 1 pt, RGB colour and at least 300 dpi. Figures should be prepared near their intended A4 display size. Captions begin with a brief whole-figure title, followed by panel definitions, sample sizes, statistics, error-bar definitions and all abbreviations needed for independent interpretation.
