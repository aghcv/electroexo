===== PAGE 32 =====
• Once ovulated, ovum is viable for ~14 hours
• Sperm are viable for 3 to 6 days
• Sperm must undergo capacitation
– Thickened cell membrane must thin out to release hydrolytic

– Takes ~ 10 hours
• Optimal window: 3 days before to 14 hours after 
ovulation
Conception

===== PAGE 33 =====
• Gestation (pregnancy) lasts an average of 266 
days from conception to childbirth
• Gestational calendar is measured from first day 
of the woman’s last menstrual period (LMP)
• Birth is predicted to occur 280 days from LMP
– 3 three month intervals called trimesters

Hormones of Pregnancy

===== PAGE 37 =====
• HCG (human chorionic gonadotropin)
– secreted by trophoblast within 9 days of conception
– replaces role of LH in sustaining corpus luteum
– prevents involution of corpus luteum
• Estrogens 
– corpus luteum is source for first 12 weeks
– placenta is source from 12th week to  birth
– promotes development of uterus
– promotes development of breast duct system 
Hormones of Pregnancy

===== PAGE 38 =====
• Progesterone 
– corpus luteum is source for first 12 weeks
– placenta is source from 12th week to  birth
– suppresses uterine contractions
– promotes development of uterus
– promotes development of breast glandular tissue
• Other endocrine glands
– thyroid gland increases 50% in size  BMR of mother
– parathyroid glands enlarge & stimulate osteoclasts to 
release additional calcium from mother’s bones
– aldosterone secretion  fluid retention & leads to  in 
mother’s blood volume

===== PAGE 40 =====
• Cleavage - mitotic divisions that occur for 3 days 
after fertilization
– Two-cell stage is reached within 30 hours
– By 72 hours a solid ball of cells has formed (morula)

days to reach full thickness
• By day 6 morula forms a hollow ball of cells called 
the bastocyst
– outer cells (trophoblast) develops into a placenta
– inner cell mass (embryoblast) develops into an embryo

The Neonate

===== PAGE 59 =====
• Immunological adaptation (natural passive immunity)
– maternal antibody, IgG, diffuses across placenta
• provides 6 months of protection from most infectious 
diseases while fetal production increases
– IgA in breast milk can protect newborn from 
gastroenteritis
The Neonate

===== PAGE 60 =====
• Thermoregulation
– Infant has larger ratio of surface area to volume
• loses heat more easily
• defenses
– brown fat deposited during weeks 17 to 20 fetal life 
(mitochondria break it down to pyruvic acid releasing only heat)
– infant grows and increases metabolic rate
– accumulates subcutaneous fat for insulation
• Fluid Balance
– Kidneys not fully developed at birth
• cannot concentrate urine so have a high rate of water loss 
and require more fluid intake, relative to body weight
Neonate

===== PAGE 61 =====
• Infants born weighing under 5.5 lb.
• Infants born before 7 months suffer from 
– respiratory distress syndrome
• insufficient surfactant causing alveolar collapse with 
exhalation
– thermoregulatory problems due to undeveloped 
hypothalamus -- keep in incubator
– digestive system not well developed must be fed low-
fat formula instead of breast milk
– immature liver fails to synthesize plasma proteins
• edema, deficiency of clotting and jaundice from bile

– vaginal discharge called lochia

Milk Production

===== PAGE 65 =====
Prolactin causes breast milk production
– at birth, prolactin secretion drops to non-pregnant 
levels
– but  20-fold after each nursing event
– without continuous nursing, production stops in 1 
week
– prolactin inhibition of GnRH supresses ovarian 
cycling
• 5-10% of women become pregnant again while nursing

===== PAGE 67 =====
Oxytocin causes breast milk ejection
– infant’s suckling stimulates sensory receptors in 
nipple
– signals the hypothalamus & posterior pituitary to 
release the hormone oxytocin
– alveolar glands contract to squeeze milk into duct
• milk flows within 30-60 seconds after suckling 
begins
Milk Ejection

===== PAGE 68 =====
• Nutrition for first 1 to 3 days after birth
• Similar to breast milk but contains 1/3 less fat, 
thinner
– newborns have sufficient fat and water reserves
• Contains IgA protection from gastroenteritis
• Has a laxative effect that clears intestine of 
meconium (green, bile-filled fecal material in 
newborn)
Colostrum

===== PAGE 69 =====
• Breast milk supplies antibodies & colonizes 
intestine with beneficial bacteria
• Nursing woman can produce 1.5L per day
–  calorie intake by 300,  Ca+2 & vitamin D
• Cow’s milk is not a good substitute for human 
milk
– 1/3 less lactose but 3 times as much protein
– harder to digest & more nitrogenous waste 
(diaper rash)
