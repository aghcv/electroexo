Functions of Blood
• Transport Respiratory Gasses (O2, CO2)
• Pickup and Deliver Nutrients (glucose, amino acids, 
fats, vitamins, minerals, water)
• Thermoregulation by conducting heat to the body 
surface as needed
• Clearing metabolic wastes- urinary blood flow
• Transporting internal secretions (e.g. hormones)
• Buffering blood hydrogen ions (acid-base balance)
• Providing generalized and specific protections
-Phagocytic blood cells
-Immune system

===== PAGE 26 =====
External Barriers (Non-specific- no prior 
exposure required)
– Skin
• Tough outer layer of keratin is dry and nutrient-poor
• Lactic acid (acid mantle) is a component of perspiration
– Mucous membranes
• Line body cavities open to the exterior
• Stickiness of mucus with antibacterial enzymes
First Line of Defense

===== PAGE 27 =====
Non-specific Internal Processes (no prior 
exposure required)
– Phagocytic cells 
• Neutrophils, monocytes, macrophages
– Inflammatory response
• Increased blood flow (hyperemia) causing redness
• Increased capillary permeability (swelling)
– Fever
• Low tolerance of pathogens to temperature elevation
– Interferons
• Released by viruses
• Target infected cells for macrophage destruction
Second Line of Defense

===== PAGE 28 =====
Immune System (Specific: prior exposure 
required)
– Exhibits specificity and memory
• Recognize
• React
• Remember
– Involves lymphocytes
– Cellular immunity mediated by cells (T cells)
– Humoral immunity mediated by antibodies (B cells)
Third Line of Defense

===== PAGE 29 =====
Antigens
• Complex molecules
– Structure unique to each person
– Proteins, polysaccharides, glycoproteins, glycolipids
• Trigger an immune response 
• Epitope (antigenic determinant)
– Part of an antigen that stimulates immune responses
Antigens

===== PAGE 30 =====
• Fetal stem cells from red bone marrow
• Mature in thymus
– thymosins stimulate maturing T cells to 
produce antigen receptors
– immunocompetent T cell has antigen 
receptors in place
• Deployment
– naïve T cells colonize lymphatic tissue and 
organs
Life Cycle of T Cells

===== PAGE 31 =====
T cells are exposed to “self” antigens within 
the thymus gland
– Cells that react (98%)
• destroyed or inactivated
– Cells that do not react (2%) 
• form clones with identical receptors for a specific 
antigen
• constitute naïve lymphocyte pool
• colonize lymphatic tissue and organs throughout 
the body.
Selection of T Cell Lymphocytes

===== PAGE 32 =====
• Fetal stem cells remain in red bone marrow
• Undergo same selection as T cells
• Self-tolerant B cells form B cell clones
– synthesize receptors for their antigens, divide rapidly, 
produce immunocompetent clones
• Deploy to lymphatic tissue (lymph nodes, 
spleen), mucus membranes and other organs
Selection of B Cell Lymphocytes

Note: the “self” identifying proteins on the surfaces of 
most of our cells are referred to as MHC-I

===== PAGE 42 =====
Plasma cells
• Larger than B cells
• Develop mainly in lymph nodes
• Only about 10% remain there
• Produce antibodies until they die
– Produce about 2,000 per second
– Cells survive for 4 or 5 days
Plasma Cells

===== PAGE 43 =====
Antibodies
• Form classes of immunoglobulin proteins
– IgA, IgD, IgE, IgG, IgM
• Attack by
– Neutralization (mask antigen pathogenic site)
– Agglutination (clumping enemy cells to immobilize)
– Precipitation (antigens settle out and phagocytized)
– Compliment fixation (compliment proteins undergo 
reaction pathways leading to destruction of 
pathogenic cells)
