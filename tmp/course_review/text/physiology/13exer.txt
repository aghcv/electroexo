===== PAGE 8 =====
Fueling Exercise

===== PAGE 9 =====
Pupil dilates to allow more light access to rods and cones.
Salivary gland secretions are reduced. Mouth becomes dry.
Heart rate and strength of beat increase providing added blood flow for
oxygen delivery.
Airways dilate and lung ventilation increases bringing more oxygen to the
lungs.
Liver releases stored glucose into the blood for use as fuel for energy
production.
Adrenal glands release the hormones norepinephrine and epinephrine that
reinforce the sympathetic response.
Kidneys produce less urine as blood flow to them is reduced.
Urinary bladder walls relax and sphincter tightens retaining urine.
Digestive system activity is inhibited. Secretion and motility decrease.
Blood vessels divert flow away from abdominal organs to skeletal muscle.
Arrector pili muscles of skin constrict causing goose bumps.
Sweat glands, particularly those of armpit and groin, increase secretory
activity

===== PAGE 13 =====
For strenuous exercise in healthy persons:
• At exhaustion, ventilation is still only ~ 65% maximum.
‒ Thus, the limiting factor in endurance exercise is blood 
flow needed to carry oxygen from lungs to tissues.
• Cardiac output (blood flow from the heart) can 
increase 5-fold.
• Blood flow to skeletal muscles can increase 25-fold.
‒ Thus, a redistribution of flow must also occur.

===== PAGE 14 =====
Distribution of Blood Flow

===== PAGE 15 =====
During strenuous exercise:
• Blood flow to the gut and kidneys is cut in half
• Blood flow to the skin increases ~ 6-fold
‒ to dissipate increased body heat to the environment
• Blood flow to the heart increases ~4-fold
‒ to fuel increased cardiac output
• Blood flow to skeletal muscles increases more than 
10-fold
‒ to deliver glucose and oxygen for aerobic respiration
‒ but blood flow is still the limiting factor

===== PAGE 23 =====
Thermoregulation
Increase in ATP production also increases
metabolic heat production. In response:
• Skin vessels vasodilate bringing heat to surface
• Sweat production increases
• As much a 5 -10 pounds of water (and salt) 
can be lost on a hot day.
After exercise:
• ADH promotes water reabsorption to correct for 
dehydration
• Aldosterone promotes salt and water reabsorption 
to correct for volume depletion
