Digestive Wastes

===== PAGE 2 =====
• About 1500 ml of chyme passes into the colon per day
• Nearly all Na+ and Cl- are efficiently reabsorbed
• Electrolyte uptake promotes osmotic reabsorption of water
• Proximal colon is the absorptive colon
• Populated with colonic bacteria
• Digest small amounts of cellulose for a few extra calories
• Produce vitamins including significant amounts of vitamin K
• Contribute to flatus, especially CO2, hydrogen, methane
• Distal colon is the fecal storage colon
• About 130 gm of feces are passed each day.
• About 30 gm of solids
• About 100 ml of water

• Mass contractions of colon occur 2 or 3 times 
a day.
− Begins in transverse colon on down
− Forces contents into the rectum
− Fecal consistency can generally be sensed
− Distension of rectum initiates reflex
− External anal sphincter relaxed voluntarily
− Emptying aided by “valsalva maneuver”
− If over-ridden, contents pass back to sigmoid 
colon  constipation (?)

===== PAGE 11 =====
• Lactase is an enzyme that converts lactose 
(milk sugar) into glucose and galactose.
− Historically necessary while nursing
− Levels declined in childhood into adulthood
− Pastoral cultures continued milk consumption 
and tended to retain lactase
• No lactase  lactose passed to colon
− Increases osmotic pressure  diarrhea 
− Bacterial fermentation  cramps & flatulence
• Cultural roots in Americans
− High in Mediterranean, African and Asian Cultures
