===== PAGE 21 =====
• Stress
– caused by any situation that upsets 
homeostasis and threatens one’s physical 
or emotional well-being
• General adaptation syndrome 
– way body reacts to stress
– occurs  in 3 stages
1. alarm reaction
2. stage of resistance
3. stage of exhaustion
Adapting to Stress

===== PAGE 22 =====
This is the initial response
•  epinephrine and norepinephrine levels
•  HR and  BP
•  glycogenolysis (glycogen  glucose)
•  blood glucose levels
• Sodium and water retention (aldosterone)
Alarm Stage

===== PAGE 23 =====
After a few hours, glycogen reserves are 
gone
•  cortisol levels
•  fat and protein breakdown
•  gluconeogenesis
•  loss of protein
− depressed immune function
− susceptibility to infection
Resistance Stage

===== PAGE 24 =====
After months, fat reserves are gone
•  protein breakdown  muscle wasting 
• loss of glucose homeostasis
• hypokalemia ( K+) and alkalosis ( H+) 
• death (heart failure, kidney failure, infection)
Exhaustion Stage

===== PAGE 25 =====
Starvation
• Loss of carbohydrate reserves (day 1)
• Protein extraction from muscle
• Hunger ceases & fat metabolism  (day 3)
• Metabolism slows-  pulse,  BP, chills
• Dry skin, hair loss from extraction of protein
• Loss of antibody proteins- immune deficiency
• Mouth sores, anemia, bone degeneration
• Loss of coordination (several weeks)
• Blindness and deafness toward the end (50-70 
days)
• Death (no carbohydrate, no fat, 50% protein)

Adapting to Cold 
• Peripheral vasoconstriction
• Behavioral response
• Increase metabolic heat production
− Increasing muscle activity (shivering)
− Increasing brown fat metabolism
− Increasing thyroid hormone levels
• Contraction of arrector pilli muscles
(cutis anserina)

===== PAGE 32 =====
Adapting to Heat 
• Peripheral vasodilation
• Behavioral response
• Increase convection (fan, breeze)
Effective at all temperatures:
• Decreased activity
• Evaporation of sweat
