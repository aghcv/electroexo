COURSE DESCRIPTION- This survey course provides an over view of the functioning systems of the 
human body: the skeletal, muscular, nervous, circulatory, respiratory, endocrine, digestive, urinary and 
reproductive systems. Material covered includes feedback mechanisms that control organ system function, 
techniques used to assess and monitor physiological processes and the consequences of damage and disease. 
While the course uses a “systems” approach, consideration is given to the integration of organ system  
processes required to maintain an optimally functioning organism. 
 
COURSE GOALS are to demonstrate how physiological processes integrate the individual functions of all 
of the body’s different organ systems into a functioning whole- the human organism; to examine the feedback 
controls required to sustain normal function; to highlight basic engineering principles incorporated into the 
natural design of living organisms; and to provide examples of  experimental approaches used to study 
physiological processes and engineering approaches used to compensate for physiological deficiencies. 
 
RECOMMENDED TEXT:  Guyton, A.C. and J.E. Hall; Textbook of Medical Physiology (13th ed.) 
Philadelphia, PA: Elsevier Health Sciences, 2016 
 
GRADING- Systems Physiology is a 3 -credit lecture course. The course is divided into four seri es of 
lectures, with one exam scheduled for each series. In addition, a cumulative exam will be given along with 
the exam covering the last series of lectures, for a total of five exams. For undergraduate students, the final 
grade is the average of the fou r highest scores (the single lowest score being dropped).  A missed exam 
cannot be used as the dropped score. For graduate students, the final grade is based on the average of all five 
exams. Each of the five exams will consist of 40 questions of true and false, multiple choice or item matching 
type.  Sample exams with an answer key will be provided. Exams are scored on a percentage basis, with the 
final grade determined from the average as follows: 
   
90-100= A 87-89=B+ 80-86=B 77-79=B- 74-76=C+ 
67-73=C 64-66=C- 61-63=D+ 55-60=D <55=F
