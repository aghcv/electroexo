===== PAGE 62 =====
SYMPATHETIC
1. Nerve impulses originate from the
thoracolumbar preganglionic autonomic
fibers.
2. Synapses between the pre- and post-
ganglionic neurons are located in the
prevertebral and paravertebral ganglia.
The ganglia are interconnected making
the effects generalized.
3. The preganglionic fibers are short and the
postganglionic fibers are long.
4. Norepinephrine is the neurotransmitter at
the synapse of the postganglionic motor
nerve with the effector.
5. The sympathetic is the division of the
nervous system that comes to the aid of
the body in times of stress.
6. Metabolic energy is rapidly consumed
PARASYMPATHETIC
1. Nerve impulses originate from the
craniosacral preganglionic autonomic
fibers.
2. Synapses between the pre- and post-
ganglionic neurons are located in the
terminal ganglia. The ganglia are not
interconnected so effects are localized to
specific organ systems.
3. The preganglionic fibers are long and the
postganglionic fibers are short.
4. Acetylcholine is the neurotransmitter at
the synapse of the postganglionic motor
nerve with the effector.
5. The parasympathetic is the division of the
nervous system that takes care of us in
quiet times between stresses.
6. Metabolic energy (ATP) is conserved
Branches Compared

===== PAGE 63 =====
SYMPATHETIC
Effects on specific organs respond to stress:
a. Pupil dilates to allow more light access to rods and
cones.
b. Salivary gland secretions are reduced. Mouth
becomes dry.
c. Heart rate and strength of beat increase providing
added blood flow for oxygen delivery.
d. Airways dilate and lung ventilation increases
bringing more oxygen to the lungs.
e. Liver releases stored glucose into the blood for
use as fuel for energy production.
f. Adrenal glands release the hormones
norepinephrine and epinephrine that reinforce
the sympathetic response.
g. Kidneys produce less urine as blood flow to them
is reduced.
h. Urinary bladder walls relax and sphincter tightens
retaining urine.
i. Digestive system activity is inhibited. Secretion
and motility decrease.
j. Blood vessels divert flow away from abdominal
organs to skeletal muscle.
k. Arrector pili muscles of skin constrict causing
goose bumps.
l. Sweat glands, particularly those of armpit and
groin, increase secretory activity
PARASYMPATHETIC
Effects on specific organs are conservative:
a. Pupil constricts reducing the amount of light
entering eye.
b. Salivary gland secretions increase as food is
ingested.
c. Heart rate and strength of beat are reduced
conserving the heart at rest.
d. Airways constrict and lung ventilation is reduced
as energy is conserved.
e. Liver stores glucose in preparation for need at a
later time.
f. Adrenal glands have no innervation from the
parasympathetic nervous system. The glands
are exceptions to the “dual innervation” concept.
g. Kidneys produce urine at an appropriate rate to
clear metabolites and balance water.
h. Urinary bladder responds normally to the
micturition reflex and voluntary control.
i. Digestive system secretion and motility increase
as needed for proper digestion at mealtime.
j. Blood vessels provide appropriate flow to organs
of the digestive and urinary systems.
k. Arrector pili muscles are not innervated.
l. Sweat glands are not innervated.
