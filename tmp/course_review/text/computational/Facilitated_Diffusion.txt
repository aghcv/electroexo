✓ Simple versus facilitated diffusion: 
 
➢ Simple: The rate of diffusion across a cell membrane increases 
proportionately with the concentration or amount gradient of the neutral 
diffusing substance.  
 
➢ Facilitated diffusion: The rate of diffusion approaches a maximum, Vmax, as 
the concentration gradient of the diffusing substance increases.

Facilitated diffusion or carrier-mediated transport:  
 
 
➢ Differs from simple diffusion through open channels.  
 
➢ Requires interaction of the molecules or ions with a carrier protein that 
facilitates its passage through the membrane (conformational changes).  
 
➢ Highly selective 
 
➢ Saturable: The rate of diffusion 
approaches a maximum rate as the 
concentration of the substance in the 
donor compartment increases.  
 
 
➢ Driven by electrochemical gradient 
 
➢ Examples of substances transported via carrier-mediated transport include 
glucose (neutral, polar molecule) and amino acids.

➢ Three types of carrier-mediated transport: 
o Uniport: Carrier proteins that transport a single solute across a cell 
membrane.  
Example: Glucose transporter 
o Symport: Carrier proteins that simultaneously transport two different 
solute molecules in the SAME direction across the cell membrane.  
Example: Sodium-driven glucose symport which transports 
glucose and sodium from the lumen of the gut to the intestinal 
epithelium. 
o Antiport: Carrier proteins that simultaneously transport two different 
solute molecules in the OPPOSITE direction across the cell membrane.  
Example: Sodium-calcium exchanger in muscle and nerve cells.
