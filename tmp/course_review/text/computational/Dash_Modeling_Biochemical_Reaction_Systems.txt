Mass Conservation Equations for Biochemical Systems
Dynamic State: dC/dt =  S * Jrxn – Jtran (Mass Balance Eqs.)
C = (C1,…,CM) is the vector of metabolite concentrations
Jrxn = (Jrxn1,…,JrxnN) is the vector of metabolic reaction fluxes/rates
Jtran = (Jtran1,…,JtranN) is the vector of outward transport fluxes/rates
S = [Sij] = matrix of metabolites stoichiometric coefficients
M = number of metabolites in the system (i = 1,…,M)
N = number of reactions in the system (j = 1, …,N)
Steady State: S * Jrxn = Jtran (Flux Balance Eqs.)
In a closed system without any synthesis or degradation: Jtran = 0
Kinetic modeling of transport fluxes Jtran via channels and transporters 
(uniporters, symporters, antiporters) and reaction fluxes Jrxn via enzymes 
are key to dynamic modeling of integrated metabolic systems and gain 
mechanistic understanding of the systems.

Energy Coupling
 A spontaneous reaction may drive a non-spontaneous 
reaction through energy coupling.  
 Free energy changes of coupled reactions are additive.  
Some enzyme-catalyzed reactions are interpretable as       
two coupled half-reactions, one spontaneous and the    
other non-spontaneous. 
 At the enzyme active site, the coupled reaction is 
kinetically facilitated, while individual half-reactions  
are prevented. 
 Free energy changes of half reactions may be summed,  
to yield the free energy of the coupled reaction.

===== PAGE 12 =====
For example, in the reaction catalyzed by the glycolysis 
enzyme Hexokinase, the half-reactions are:
ATP + H2O ↔ ADP  +  Pi ,           ∆Go' = -31 kJ/mol
Pi + glucose ↔ glucose-6-P + H2O, ∆Go' = +14 kJ/mol
Coupled reaction:
ATP+ glucose ↔ ADP + glucose-6-P, ∆Go' = -17 kJ/mol
The structure of the enzyme active site, from which H2O    
is excluded, prevents the individual hydrolytic reactions,
while favoring the coupled reaction.

Enzymes
 Enzymes are catalysts, that speed up the rate of reactions, 
without changing the extent of the reactions.
 They are (in general) large proteins and are highly specific; 
usually each enzyme speeds up only a single reaction.
 They are highly regulated by a pile of things: phosphorylation, 
calcium, ATP , their own products, etc., resulting in extremely 
complex webs of intracellular biochemical reactions.
Basic problem of enzyme kinetics
Suppose an enzyme reacts with a substrate, giving a product.
S + E P + E
If we simply apply the law of mass action to this reaction, the 
rate of reaction would be a linearly increasing function of [S]. 
As [S] gets very big, so would the reaction rate. This doesn’t 
happen. In reality, the reaction rate saturates.

Positive/negative cooperativity
Usually, the binding of the 
first S changes the rate at 
which the second S binds.
• If the binding rate of the 
second S is increased, it’s 
called positive cooperativity
• If the binding rate of the 
second S is decreased, it’s 
called negative cooperativity.
