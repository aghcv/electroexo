Physiologically Based Pharmacokinetic Models (PBPK) 
 
 Physiologically based models: 
 For compartment models that we have considered so far: 
 Fluid streams entering or leaving a given compartment are NOT 
explicitly expressed.  
 
 A compartment DOES NOT correspond to a specific organ or part of 
an organ in the body. 
 
 Work well for modeling the body as a whole, since no fluid flows are 
considered to cross boundaries (e.g., modeling the plasma region as one 
compartment). 
 
  To model a local region (e.g., uptake by a specific organ), inlet and exit of 
blood flow (lymph flow is usually neglected) across the compartment 
boundaries must be explicitly specified.  
 
 This type of compartmental models is referred to physiologically 
based pharmacokinetic models (PBPK) 
 
 For PBPK models, various organs and tissues are represented by 
their corresponding volumes and blood flow rates.

 Applications of PBPK include: 
 Facilitating drug development 
 Predicting the biodistribution of drugs and their metabolites in the body. 
 Scaling up the dose and schedule from animal models (rodents) to human being. 
 Performing risk assessment of environmental chemcials 
  
 
 
 The general approach for PBPK modeling is: 
 Determine which organs are relevant for a given drug. 
 Develop mathematical models of drug transport in each organ. 
 Couple organ models at the systemic level. 
 Determine all model constants (parameters) based upon data in the literature. 
 Solve the coupled mathematical equations (ODEs) simultaneously as functions of 
time.

 Mass transport between compartments by convection (bulk fluid flow): 
 So far, we have talked about diffusion (simple and facilitated), active transport, and 
chemical transformation as mechanisms of mass transport between compartments. 
 
 Another important of mass transport is convection.  
 
Diffusion (optimal for short distances); concentration gradient 
 
Convection (optimal long distances); pressure gradient 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Burness , G.P. Science 296:1245, 2002 
 
 
 Convection (or bulk flow): 
o Is the transport of solute molecules from one part of the body to another by taking 
on the velocity of the fluid  
o Requires energy in the form of a pressure gradient.

mass/time/unit area.  
 Convective mass flow rate = cross-sectional area x convective mass flux  = average 
flow rate x solute concentration.

compartment is well mixed; in other words, axial and radial diffusions are instantaneous.

concentration leaving the compartment is assumed to be the same as that within the
