COMPARTMENTAL MODELING 
 Symbolic representation of a compartmental system or model: 
 
 A circle is used to represent a 
compartment. 
 Consider a system with n 
compartment (i = 1,…, n; j = 1, …, n) 
 
 Each arrow leaving or entering a 
compartment is referred to as the rate of flow or rate of transfer of a given 
species into or out of the compartment, where: 
o qi(t) = the amount of material in compartment i at time t 
o Fij and Fji = Respective rates of flow or rates of transfer of material 
from compartment i to compartment j and from compartment j to 
compartment i.  
o Foi = Instantaneous rate of flow of material from outside the system 
(environment) into compartment i. 
o Fio = Rate of flow or rate of transfer of material directly out of the 
system from compartment i into the environment.  
 Closed system if Fio = 0 and Foi = 0, i = 1, 2,…, n, otherwise an open 
system.

For compartment systems where inter-compartment transfer 
occurs by passing through a physical barrier (e.g., a 
biological membrane).  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
a. Transport properties of a biological membrane such as a cell membrane. 
i. Cell membranes are dynamic and selective structures (~ 7.5 to 
10 m thick), which regulate the movement of molecules (e.g., 
glucose) and ions (e.g., Na+, K+, Cl) in and out of cells. 
 
ii. Cell membranes consist almost entirely of a lipid (phospholipid) 
bilayer (2 molecules thick) with large number of protein molecules 
floating in the lipid, many penetrating all the way through.  
iii. Each lipid molecule has a hydrophilic head and a hydrophobic tail.

iv. Few substances can penetrate the lipid bilayer. The lipid 
solubility of a substance determines its permeability across the 
lipid bilayer. 
a. Lipid-soluble molecules such as O2, N2, alcohols readily 
dissolve in the lipid bilayer.  
 
b. Small neutral polar molecules such as H2O, CO2, urea can 
penetrate the lipid bilayer because of their much smaller 
size and neutral charge.  
 
v. The lipid bilayer forms a barrier for the movement of most 
water-soluble (hydrophilic) substances and charged ions and 
molecules between the extracellular fluid and the intracellular 
fluid.

vi. Protein molecules: 
- Penetrate the lipid bilayer all the way through.  
- Have entirely different transport properties and provide 
alternative pathway through the cell membrane, and hence play 
an important role in membrane fluidity.  
- Many are called transport proteins.  
a. Channel proteins:  
i. Watery spaces all the way through and allow 
free movement of certain ions or molecules 
(e.g., Na+, K+, H2O).   
ii. For ion channels, the driving force is an 
electrochemical gradient across the cell membrane 
(ion concentration gradient + voltage gradient 
across the cell membrane (40 to 90 mv))  
iii. Do not exhibit saturability of their binding 
sites.

1. Passive processes 
a. Diffusion 
i. Simple diffusion: the substance is driven by concentration difference 
across the membrane (Fick’s law) or electrochemical gradient via an 
ion channel (Nernst-Planck equation). 
 
ii. Facilitated (or carrier-mediated) diffusion: Requires interaction of 
the molecule or ion with a carrier protein that facilitates its passage 
through the cell membrane (e.g. glucose transport).  
2. Active processes:  
i. Movement of ions or molecules across the cell membrane in 
combination with a carrier protein against an energy gradient (e.g. 
electrochemical gradient) such as from a low concentration region to 
a high concentration region. 
 
ii. Requires the expenditure of energy (adenosine tri-phosphate, ATP).
